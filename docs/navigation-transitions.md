## Reading the tables

Coordinates are (x,y,plane). **Standing node** means a tile from which the player interacts; **object target** is the object's world location or the script's named landmark. The two must not be substituted for one another. A dynamically queried script landmark does not establish the exact returned object.world or an object's footprint. Sources below distinguish those cases.

For ordinary ladders, C(x,y,p) denotes the four cardinal neighbours of (x,y,p), on plane p. A listed A ↔ B covers both directions only where both source rows exist. The arrival column supplies the representative route node; the adjacent text records other sourced destinations or the script's observed-state acceptance area. None of those predicates constitutes an observed success in this research run.

Durations are **upstream tick metadata**, not measured elapsed time. A dash means unspecified. The parser leaves an ordinary transport's blank duration at zero; its transport node then adds that value to route cost. Zero therefore cannot be presented as an observed instantaneous crossing. Network duration is the maximum of the origin and destination entries, and their quest requirements are combined. Bare quest names mean FINISHED. [Duration parsing][duration], [network merge][merge], [quest parsing][quest-states], [route cost][transport-cost].

P11's chosen planner cost is a conservative **80 units per semantic click or selection**, matching the existing door interaction scale. Thus a single ladder interaction costs 80 and a transport interaction followed by a destination selection costs 160. This is an explicit planner policy, separate from upstream duration metadata and future live timing measurements.

Waydar and Lumdo use a base conversation-edge cost of 160. Their variable number of continue pages is not encoded as a guessed duration. Travel conversation replies use the account's reading delay and native mouse input, with the captured speaker, text and complete choice list rechecked before dispatch.

“No encoded gate” means the cited rows specify no skill, item, currency, quest, varbit, or varplayer requirement. It does not certify all account states. These are member-quest routes; the ordinary TSV rows generally leave the isMembers field blank. Adding a members-world restriction would therefore be a catalog policy rather than a value recovered from those rows. Quest loadouts, food, prayers, and puzzle items must not become invented ladder requirements.

## Implemented transport boundary

The catalog currently admits 56 directed entries from these tables. Object, NPC, fixed-widget and scoped destination-label steps call the existing native input owners. Runtime snapshots verify the arrival area and plane; a dispatched input alone never proves arrival. Waydar and Lumdo retain their supported conversations, including Waydar's exact Yes/Yes. choice and two observed closed-dialogue ticks after arrival. Foreign speakers or choices interrupt the journey and return the dialogue to Lua. Area, poison and other safety interrupts retain priority over the owned conversation.

An interrupted continuation carries the selected transport, attempted steps, remaining active-tick budget and failed service groups. Input time and emergency pauses are excluded; handler time cannot reset the remaining budget. A previously issued climb waits for its landing instead of being repeated. Scene loading can invalidate the public snapshot without losing the native input outcome; only a fresh frame establishes arrival or drives replanning.

The offline audit exercises all 56 selected edges and their passable standing/arrival nodes, plus the 13 existing routes under both unavailable quest data and an explicit supported quest profile. Native mouse/menu tests verify object world/action binding, NPC selection, scoped destination labels, dialogue identity, hidden widgets and cancellation. These checks do not establish live transport success. Femi's toll branch, unproved landings, initial quest conversations, puzzles and resource teleports remain in Lua pending their required evidence or quest-owned decisions.
